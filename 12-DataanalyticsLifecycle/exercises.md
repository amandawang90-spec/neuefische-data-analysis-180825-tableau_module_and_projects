# Question 3: Tableau Ethics Exercises – Misleading Visualizations
These exercises help you recognize and fix common ethical visualization issues in Tableau.  
Follow the instructions carefully and reflect on what could be misleading or unethical in each case.

By recreating these two simple but deceptive visuals, you’ll experience how small design decisions — missing labels, limited context, or unclear metrics — can mislead your audience.  
After building each chart, ask:  
- Does the visualization give full context?  
- Could the audience misinterpret the data?  
- What simple changes make it more transparent?

## Exercise 1: Hidden Baselines
- Create a **bar chart**:
   - Drag `Sub-Category` to Rows.
   - Drag `SUM(Sales)` to Columns.
- **Sort** the bars in **descending order** by Sales.
- **Filter** the view to show only the **Top 5 Sub-Categories**.
- Hide the filter shelf to make the chart look complete.
- Add a title such as: **“Top Sub-Categories by Sales”** (but don’t specify “Top 5” in the title yet).
- What is misleading in this chart and how can we fix?

## Exercise 2: Cumulative Misinterpretation
Using the **Superstore** dataset:
   - Drag `Order Date` to Columns → set it to **Month**(continuos) or **Quarter**(continuos).
   - Drag `SUM(Sales)` to Rows.
   - Right-click → **Quick Table Calculation → Running Total**.
- What is misleading in this chart and how can we fix?
